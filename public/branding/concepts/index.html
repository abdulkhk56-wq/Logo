Three directions for the SAFI symbol

Every mark here is built with compass and straight-edge on a 6-unit grid and drawn in black only. Colour, final typography and the wider identity are held back until one symbol is approved.

GeometryEvery edge is a circle from the construction drawing or a straight line. There are no freehand curves.
SilhouetteEach mark is tested as one solid black shape, at 24px and 16px, without the wordmark.
PresentationNo colour, gradients, shadows or mockups. The provisional wordmark is the same for all three.

Shared construction

The flame primitive

All three concepts integrate the same flame in different ways. It is drawn once, from five circles and one line.

  1. Artboard 120 × 120, module u = 6 (a 20 × 20 grid). Base line at y = 108.
  2. Two flank circles of radius 96 (16u), centred at (12, 84) and (108, 84). Their lens is the flame body, 96 wide.
  3. Main tip T is where the right flank crosses x = 72 (12u): T = (72, 9.1). Secondary tip T₂ is where the left flank crosses x = 36 (6u): T₂ = (36, 20.5).
  4. Notch N = (48, 60), on the grid. Two concave circles carve it: radius 72 (12u) through N and T, and radius 48 (8u) through N and T₂.
  5. Cut the lens flat at the base line. Round both feet with radius 12 (2u), tangent to base and flank.

Why two tongues: a single pointed shape with a round base reads as a drop of oil. A second, lower tongue is what makes a flame a flame. The flat base stops it reading as a drop and sets it on a level.

Concept 1

Tandem

One flame, one channel

1 · Construction

  1. Start from the flame primitive (shared construction, above).
  2. Draw two concentric circles at grid point C = (0, 96), radii 45 and 51. The 6-unit band between them (1u) is the channel.
  3. Subtract the band. The flame divides into an outer blade (main tongue) and an inner blade (secondary tongue).

8 construction circles · 9 arcs · 2 straight edges · module u = 6

2 · Black-and-white logo

3 · Meaning

The outer blade is the process: energy, throughput, the plant. The inner blade is the operator. The channel is the boundary SAFI maintains between them: a filtered path where noise is removed before it reaches the person. Two parts, one flame: a co-pilot, not a replacement.

Critique

  • The outer silhouette sits in the same family as well-known consumer flame icons (fire emoji, dating apps). Once the channel closes, which it does below 24px, that generic silhouette is all that remains.
  • The channel carries all the meaning, and it is the first thing to disappear when the mark is reduced.
  • Construction is clean: 9 arcs and 2 straight edges, all from 8 circles.

Concept 2

Laminar

The flame read through plates

1 · Construction

  1. Start from the flame primitive.
  2. Lay three vertical plates over it on the module: width 24 (4u), gap 12 (2u), from x = 12 to x = 108. The plate edges fall on the primitive's key points: x = 36 on the secondary tip T₂, x = 48 on the notch N, x = 72 on the main tip T.
  3. Keep only what lies inside the plates. Each plate inherits its top from the flame and ends in one arc and one sharp corner: the outer plates take the flank arcs, and the centre plate takes the notch-to-tip arc. The notch arc to T₂ falls entirely in a gap.

6 construction circles · 7 arcs · 7 straight edges · module u = 6

2 · Black-and-white logo

3 · Meaning

Three plates are a filter element, a heat-exchanger plate pack and a row of level bars on an HMI, all at once. The flame exists only where they agree. Energy that has passed through structure is controlled, laminar and measurable. The flame is never drawn: it appears in the collective outline of the plates.

Critique

  • At a glance it can be read as three blades, a bar chart or a tulip. The flame reading depends on the centre plate's rising edge, so plate count and gaps must stay fixed.
  • The 12-unit gaps become 1.6px at 16px. They hold in the test below, but this is the limit; a dedicated favicon cut may be needed.
  • Most disciplined of the three: one primitive and one rule (intersection with equal plates). 7 arcs and 7 straight edges.

Concept 3

Gate

The flame as the counter of a block

1 · Construction

  1. Draw the block: a 120 × 120 square, the full 20u artboard.
  2. Scale the flame primitive to 10/16 (base width 60 = 10u) and stand it on the block's base line.
  3. Subtract it. The flame becomes the counter, opening at the base, and the block becomes the structure that contains it.

6 construction circles · 6 arcs · 5 straight edges · module u = 6

2 · Black-and-white logo

3 · Meaning

The block is the operating envelope: the limits the operators define. The flame burns inside it, rising from the base and never touching the walls. Stability is the dominant form, and energy is the space that it holds.

Critique

  • A flame cut out of a square is a common icon pattern (fire-safety signage, generic 'burn' icons). This is its weakest point, and it is a serious one for a corporate mark.
  • It is an emblem rather than a symbol: it needs its container, and in a lockup the solid square outweighs the wordmark.
  • It is the strongest at 16px: one solid shape with one counter.

Recommendation

Develop Concept 2, Laminar

Laminar is the only direction where the flame is integrated rather than drawn. There is no flame outline anywhere in the mark: three equal plates, cut by one construction, produce it. That is what makes it ownable.

  • Originality. Tandem and Gate both end in silhouettes that already exist: a flame icon, and a flame cut out of a square. Laminar's silhouette of three rising plates is not a stock shape.
  • Industrial credibility. Plates are the vocabulary of the industry: filter elements, plate heat exchangers, separator internals and HMI level bars. It looks engineered because it is built the way engineered parts are.
  • It covers the brief without decoration. Energy is the flame outline. Filtration and purification are the plates and the gaps between them. Control and stability are equal plates, a common base and a fixed rhythm.
  • Scalability. It still reads as three rising forms at 16px. Tandem's meaning lives in a channel that disappears at that size.
  • Not chosen for complexity. Gate is simpler and scores higher on scalability. It loses on originality, which matters most for a corporate symbol.

Before colour and identity

Approve or redirect the symbol first. On approval, the next step is optical refinement: tip overshoot, gap compensation at small sizes and a dedicated 16px cut. Only then come the final wordmark (drawn to the plate rhythm), the palette and the complete identity.
